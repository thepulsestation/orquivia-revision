From a change request to a traceable decision

Recording a request, its decision and the affected version makes the required work and delivery implications visible. Automating that workflow must preserve the approval process.

In this article

01

Separate the request, the decision and the work

A received request is not an approved modification. The record needs to distinguish what is requested, which documents it affects, who must review it and what was decided. This prevents an informal comment from becoming an ambiguous instruction, or a partial approval from being treated as permission to implement the entire change.

02

The context must travel with the change

Alongside the request, retain the project, document reference, starting version and date. The responsible person records the authorised scope and its expected effect on delivery. If that effect is not yet known, the status should identify it as awaiting assessment, rather than turn an estimate into a committed date.

A common identifier links the request to its tasks and final documentation. If the decision changes, preserving its history avoids overwriting the reason for work already completed. This makes it possible to check which version responds to each instruction and what remains outstanding, without relying on the memory of those involved in the conversation.

03

Automate the workflow, preserve professional judgement

An app can capture the request, assign a review and flag a missing response. Once an authorised decision is recorded, it can create tasks and link the new version. Technical feasibility, scope approval and scheduling decisions remain with the responsible people. Automation makes the decision visible and actionable; it does not make that decision for them.

04

Which outcome is worth checking

The value becomes clear when answering what is authorised, what remains pending and which delivery may be affected. During a pilot, record the time needed to reconstruct a request and the clarifications required before implementation. Compare this with requests of similar complexity afterwards. This shows whether the team coordinates more precisely and the client receives more consistent responses.

05

A manageable first step

Choose a recent modification and follow its path from the first message to the delivered version. Identify where context was lost or something had to be confirmed again. Orquivia can analyse that workflow and implement a record connected to existing tools, starting with one type of request and its essential statuses.
