Documents · 3 MIN

Outstanding items need a date too.

The delivery date is set, but a piece of information is missing. The problem is not always that someone is late: sometimes nobody can clearly see what was requested, who should respond, or how long it has been outstanding. Organising that workflow makes the blocker visible and gives action a clear starting point.

Sending the email does not resolve the item

Imagine a team preparing a delivery that requires documents from several people. Requests go out by email and responses arrive at different times. To check whether everything is ready, someone goes back through messages, folders and notes.

They may find the file without knowing whether it is complete or reviewed. While that gets clarified, the final preparation loses breathing room and the deadline keeps approaching.

A shared status is missing

Repeated email chasing is the symptom. The cause may be a tracking process that treats requested, received, reviewed and ready for delivery as the same thing. They are different states: receiving a file does not mean the requirement is resolved.

Improvement starts with defining the condition that allows progress, who can confirm it, and which deliverable depends on it. A long list without these relationships can still leave blockers hidden.

A request with a visible trail

An automation can record each request with its owner, required date and associated deliverable. Receipt updates its status and stores the file in the intended location. If a response is missing, a reminder identifies the outstanding item and its context.

When the content requires technical judgement, the appropriate professional remains responsible for review and approval. The system organises the steps and makes the wait visible; automatic receipt is not technical approval.

The deliverable is the reference point

For a pilot, record the time between request, receipt and review, how many outstanding items are discovered at the last stage, and which deliveries are held up by missing information. Then compare an equivalent workflow. Value comes from gaining room to deliver, not from sending more reminders. Also check that the team interprets statuses consistently and that each reminder reaches the appropriate person.

Choose a recent delivery

Take a delivery that required chasing information and reconstruct only that workflow: request, response, review and the effect on timing. Orquivia can analyse it and propose automated tracking for that document type, with proportionate reminders and a clear view of what still prevents progress.
