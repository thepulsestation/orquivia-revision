Changes that fit · 2 MIN

One small first change. A clear way to move forward.

One initial process provides a concrete test of an improvement’s value. Defining what should change makes it possible to decide from evidence whether to expand it.

Define what you want to achieve

An improvement is easier to assess when it describes an observable situation. For example: anyone on the team can check the status of a review without reconstructing a chain of messages.

Define a first pathway

Choose a process, a type of project or a specific review. Identify the people who know it and define which part to test. A bounded scope makes learning possible while work continues.

Observe before and after

Record a baseline using available data: how long an approval takes, how often a task is repeated or how many questions it requires. After the trial, compare the same signals. The aim is to learn from the change.

Keep what works and adjust

A trial does not mean everything must be rolled out. Review what worked, what added effort and what should be simplified. Documenting the resulting workflow lets the team own the improvement and develop it with the next project.
